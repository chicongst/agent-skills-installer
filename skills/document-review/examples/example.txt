# Example: /document-review

## User Input (document to review)

```markdown
# Deployment Process

Our deployment process is designed to be robust and seamless, leveraging
best-in-class tooling to empower the team.

## Steps

1. Merge your PR.
2. The pipeline runs.
3. Verify the deploy looks good.
4. Notify the team.

## Rollback

If something goes wrong, roll back.

## Notes

Deployments happen twice a day. The release window is 10:00-11:00.
Only the on-call engineer can approve a release. See the runbook for details.
Deploys are limited to one per day to reduce risk.
```

## Skill Output

# Document Review: Deployment Process

**Type**: Process guideline
**Audience**: assumed — newcomer with zero project context (not stated in the doc)

## Summary
This document reads as a summary of a process the author already knows, not as
instructions someone else can follow. Three of the four steps are not executable as
written, the rollback section contains no procedure, and the Notes section
contradicts itself on deploy frequency. Not safe to hand to a new engineer.

## Critical

### Step 3 cannot be executed — no definition of "looks good"
**Location**: Steps > item 3
**Problem**: "Verify the deploy looks good" gives the reader no criteria. A newcomer
has no way to tell success from a partial failure, so they will either approve a
broken deploy or block a healthy one.
**Fix**: Replace with concrete checks:
```
3. Verify the deploy:
   - Health endpoint returns 200: `curl https://api.example.com/health`
   - Error rate in the dashboard stays below 1% for 5 minutes after deploy
   - No new entries in the error log for the deployed service
```

### Rollback section contains no procedure
**Location**: Rollback
**Problem**: "If something goes wrong, roll back" is a restatement of the section
title. During an incident the reader needs the command, the decision threshold, and
who to tell — none are present. This is the section most likely to be read under
pressure and it is empty.
**Fix**: Specify the trigger, the command, and the verification:
```
Roll back when the error rate exceeds 5% or the health check fails twice.

1. Run `deploy rollback --service <name> --to <previous-tag>`
2. Confirm the health endpoint returns 200
3. Post in #incidents with the service name and the tag you rolled back to
```

### Internal contradiction on deploy frequency
**Location**: Notes
**Problem**: "Deployments happen twice a day" and "Deploys are limited to one per day"
directly contradict each other. A reader cannot determine the actual policy, and the
two sentences sit four lines apart.
**Fix**: Keep one statement. If both are true under different conditions, state the
condition: "Scheduled deploys run twice daily (10:00 and 16:00). Hotfix deploys are
limited to one per day outside those windows."

## Major

### Step 2 hides the entire automated process
**Location**: Steps > item 2
**Problem**: "The pipeline runs" tells the reader nothing about duration, what the
pipeline does, or how to tell it failed. A newcomer will not know whether waiting
20 minutes is normal or a sign of a hung job.
**Fix**: "The CI pipeline runs (~12 min): tests, build, staging deploy, smoke tests.
Watch it at [link]. A red X on any stage stops the deploy — see Rollback."

### "Release window" and "approval" are stated but not wired into the steps
**Location**: Notes vs Steps
**Problem**: The Notes say only the on-call engineer can approve a release and that
there is a 10:00-11:00 window, but neither constraint appears in the numbered steps.
A reader following the steps in order will merge outside the window with no approval.
**Fix**: Move both constraints into the steps as a precondition before item 1.

### Undefined reference: "the runbook"
**Location**: Notes
**Problem**: "See the runbook for details" points at a document the reader cannot
find. No name, no link.
**Fix**: Link it directly, or remove the sentence.

### Step 4 has no recipient or channel
**Location**: Steps > item 4
**Problem**: "Notify the team" does not say which team, in which channel, or what to
include.
**Fix**: "Post in #deploys: service name, version tag, and a link to the pipeline run."

## Minor

### Opening paragraph is marketing filler
**Location**: Intro
**Fix**: Delete "robust and seamless, leveraging best-in-class tooling to empower the
team." Replace with the document's actual purpose: "How to deploy a service to
production and roll back if it fails."

### Purpose and scope are never stated
**Location**: (document-wide)
**Fix**: Add one line under the title naming which services this process covers and
who it is for.

## What Works
- The numbered-step format is the right shape for this content — it only needs the
  missing detail filled in
- Separating Rollback into its own section makes it findable during an incident

## Top Priorities
1. Write the actual rollback procedure — command, threshold, notification
2. Replace "looks good" in step 3 with concrete verification checks
3. Resolve the once-vs-twice daily deploy contradiction
4. Move the release window and on-call approval into the numbered steps
5. Expand step 2 with pipeline duration and failure signal

## Verdict
**Not ready** — Three Critical findings mean a new engineer following this document
would deploy without approval and be unable to roll back.
